# "The Tower That Was Saved by a War" — script v1 (example made with the cartoon-3d-short skill)
Format: ~68 s vertical short, 202 words (~3.0 words/s including pauses), one steady narrator, our own characters.

## Facts (verify before recording)
- Tower of Pisa (campanile of Pisa Cathedral). Construction began in August 1173.
- It began to sink and lean in 1178, after the third floor was built: shallow foundations on soft clay, sand and silt.
- War (Pisa against Genoa, Lucca and Florence) halted building for almost a century. The pause let the soil
  consolidate; historians and engineers agree that without it the tower would very likely have fallen.
- When work resumed (1272, Giovanni di Simone) the upper floors were built taller on one side to compensate,
  so the tower is curved. Completed with the bell chamber in 1372.
- Closed to the public January 1990 (lean about 5.5°). Stabilised 1993-2001, mainly by soil extraction from under
  the north (high) side; straightened by about 45 cm. Reopened December 2001.
Source: Wikipedia, "Leaning Tower of Pisa"; Burland, Jamiolkowski & Viggiani, "Leaning Tower of Pisa: Behaviour after Stabilization Operations" (2009).

**Everyday analogy:** An old mattress firms up where it's been slept on; soft soil squeezed for a century firms up too.

## Script
The most famous mistake in Italy was saved by a war.
It's 1173, and Pisa is rich, proud, and building the most beautiful bell tower in the world.
Five years in, they finish the third floor. And the tower starts to lean.
The ground underneath is soft clay and sand. It's like building on a wet sponge.
Then Pisa goes to war, and the builders down tools. For almost a hundred years.
Luckily, that was the best thing that could have happened. Unluckily, nobody knew it at the time.
While the tower sat waiting, the soil slowly squeezed out its water and got firmer. Like a mattress that's been slept on for a century.
Without that break, the tower would have toppled before it was ever finished.
When the builders came back, they tried to fix the lean by making the new floors taller on the short side. So the tower isn't just leaning. It's bent, like a banana.
By 1990 it was leaning so far that they shut it. Engineers spent eleven years pulling soil out from under the high side, until it sank back about forty-five centimetres.
Not straight, of course. Nobody comes to Pisa to see a straight tower.
