# "The Day It Rained Whale" — script v1 (example made with the cartoon-3d-short skill)
Format: ~69 s vertical short, 195 words (~2.8 words/s including pauses), one steady narrator, our own characters.

## Facts (verify before recording)
- 12 November 1970, Florence, Oregon: a dead sperm whale about 45 ft long, roughly 8 tons, washed up on the beach.
- Oregon's beaches were legally state highways, so the Oregon State Highway Division had to deal with it.
- It decided to blow it up with half a ton (20 cases) of dynamite, expecting small pieces that scavenging gulls would eat.
- The blast threw large chunks of blubber over spectators hundreds of feet away; one crushed a parked car.
  Its owner had bought it in a dealership promotion called "Get a Whale of a Deal". No one was injured.
- Reporter Paul Linnman's KATU footage became famous. The gulls were scared off by the noise.
- Oregon now buries or tows beached whales. In 2020, Florence named a new park "Exploding Whale Memorial Park" by public vote.
Source: Wikipedia, "Exploding whale" (and its citations to KATU and The Oregonian).

**Everyday analogy:** A firecracker under a soup can: the can flies, the pavement stays put. A blast goes where it's easiest.

## Script
In 1970, Oregon got rid of a dead whale by blowing it up. It did not go away.
A forty-five foot sperm whale has washed up on the beach in Florence, Oregon. It weighs eight tons, and it smells.
In Oregon, the beach counts as a highway. So it's a job for the highway department.
Their plan: half a ton of dynamite. The whale will break into tiny pieces, and the seagulls will clean up the rest.
A crowd gathers. A TV crew starts rolling. Everybody steps back to a safe distance.
Boom.
Luckily, the explosion worked. Unluckily, the explosion worked.
One piece flattens a brand new car. Its owner had just bought it, at a sale called Get a Whale of a Deal.
A blast always goes where it's easiest. The sand underneath pushes back. The air above doesn't. Put a firecracker under a soup can: the can flies, the pavement stays put.
So most of the whale went up, and came straight back down. And the seagulls? They were scared off by the bang.
Today Oregon buries its whales. And in 2020, the town voted to name a park: Exploding Whale Memorial Park.
